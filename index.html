The problem

Invoices in email. Receipts in a camera roll. A voucher date on the calendar.

Income is uneven

1099-NEC, 1099-K, and unpaid invoices sit in different places. Pending work still belongs in a planning figure. This file logs Paid and Pending on the same Income tab.

Schedule C is picky

Groceries mixed with client dinners do not survive a category review. Expenses uses Schedule C–style labels, and meals follow an editable deductible percent on Setup.

2026 has two mileage rates

Business standard mileage is 72.5¢/mi from January 1–June 30 (IRS IR-2025-128) and 76¢/mi from July 1–December 31 (IRS IR-2026-29). Both cells are yellow and user-editable.

FAQ

Straight answers

Is this tax advice?

No. Nothing on this site or in the workbook is legal, tax, or accounting advice. We are not your CPA, EA, or attorney. We do not prepare returns and we do not represent you before the IRS. Use Form 1040-ES, Pub. 505, and a tax professional.

Will SEND THIS QUARTER match Form 1040-ES?

Not necessarily, and we do not claim it will. The workbook omits QBID, Additional Medicare Tax, NIIT, AMT, most credits, self-employed health insurance, retirement contributions (unless you type a number into Other deductions), annualized-income installments, and several other items. Required annual in the file follows the lesser of a 90% current-year path and a 100%/110% prior-year path as a convenience. That is not your IRS bill. How the workup is structured.

Excel and Google Sheets?

Yes. Excel: open the .xlsx, then File → Save As a working copy. Google Sheets: upload to Drive, open, then File → Make a copy. Named ranges, SUMIF, and SUMPRODUCT are Sheets-safe. Sheet protection may not transfer; yellow still means type, blue-gray still means formula. No macros and no VBA.

What if the IRS changes a 2026 rate after I buy?

Edit the yellow cells on Setup. Mileage, brackets, the Social Security wage base, and the standard deduction are not hidden in code. Mileage sources: IRS IR-2025-128 (72.5¢, Jan 1–Jun 30 2026) and IRS IR-2026-29 (76¢, Jul 1–Dec 31 2026), both user-editable.

Do you file or pay the IRS for me?

No. When you decide to pay estimated tax, pay the IRS yourself. Two electronic options the IRS describes: IRS Direct Pay and EFTPS. Those links are how to pay the IRS, not a filing service we operate.
