Expenses tab · 13 labels

Schedule C–style expense categories

These are the dropdown names in Contractor Ledger. They follow simplified Schedule C line labels so a year of receipts is not a pile of “misc.” They are not a ruling that a cost is deductible.

Schedule C is the IRS profit-or-loss form for most sole proprietors. Contractor Ledger is not Schedule C, does not prepare Schedule C, and does not decide ordinary-and-necessary, capitalization, or mixed-use questions. Keep receipts. Hand the log to a tax professional, or sit down with the IRS instructions yourself.

On the Expenses tab: date, vendor, category, amount. A formula writes Deductible. For every category except Meals, deductible = amount. For Meals, deductible = amount × the Setup meals percent (yellow cell, pre-filled 50%, labeled as a user assumption — not a 2026 inflation figure). Nothing is auto-halved without that cell.

Advertising

Client-facing promotion you choose to log as advertising rather than a general office cost. The file does not split personal/business for you.

Car and truck expenses

Actual vehicle costs (gas, repairs, and similar) if you are not using the standard mileage method for that vehicle and period. If you log miles on the Mileage tab, do not also put the same car’s actual costs here. The workbook will not stop you; a note on both tabs warns you.

Contract labor

Amounts paid to other contractors. Issuing 1099s, worker classification, and payroll are outside this file.

Depreciation

A number you type after you (or a pro) have a depreciation figure. The workbook is not a depreciation engine and does not compute section 179 or bonus depreciation.

Insurance

Business insurance premiums you choose to log here. Self-employed health insurance is not modeled as its own adjustment unless you put a figure in Setup → Other deductions — and even then it is just a number you typed.

Legal and professional services

Accountants, lawyers, bookkeepers, and similar professional fees you treat as a business expense.

Office expense

Consumable office costs that are not supplies, rent, or utilities in your own sorting habit. The label is for your consistency, not an IRS reclassification.

Rent or lease

Business rent or equipment leases you choose to record. Home-office exclusive-use tests are not in this file.

Supplies

Materials and supplies used in the work. Inventory / COGS accounting is not modeled.

Travel

Business travel costs other than meals. Meals while traveling still belong in Meals so the Setup percent can apply.

Meals

The only category with a haircut in the file. Deductible = amount × Setup meals percent. Pre-filled 50% because Schedule C meals are generally 50% deductible; the cell is a user-editable assumption. If a meal is fully non-deductible, do not log it, or set the percent with your tax pro’s guidance — we do not give that guidance.

Utilities

Business utilities you allocate yourself. The file does not compute a home-office percentage.

Other expenses

A catch-all with a notes column. Use it when a cost does not fit the labels above, then review it before you copy totals onto a return.

Mileage is a separate tab on purpose

Standard mileage lives on Mileage, not in the Expenses dropdown. Enter date, miles, and a business purpose you would recognize later. Amount = miles × the official IRS business rate for that date:

PeriodBusiness rateSource
Jan 1–Jun 30, 2026 72.5¢ per mile IRS IR-2025-128, via the standard mileage rates page
Jul 1–Dec 31, 2026 76¢ per mile IRS IR-2026-29, same IRS page

Both rates are yellow on Setup. Rows dated before July 1, 2026 use the first-half rate; on or after July 1 they use the second-half rate. Do not claim both standard mileage and actual car expenses for the same vehicle and period.

Income is also a separate tab

Income types in the file: 1099-NEC, 1099-K, 1099-MISC, Invoice/other, Other. Paid vs Pending. Pending amounts count toward the Quarterly estimate because they are expected. Do not paste W-2 wages into Income; optional Social Security wages belong on Setup so they can reduce the remaining SE wage base.

What to do with the totals

At year-end, export or print the logs. Give them to a tax professional, or use them while completing Schedule C and Form 1040-ES yourself. The Quarterly tab’s SEND THIS QUARTER figure is an organizer convenience, not a substitute for those forms. How the quarterly workup is structured.
