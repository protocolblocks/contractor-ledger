How to calculate quarterly taxes as a freelancer

A map of the published IRS structure, plus what a $29 organizer will and will not do. This is not a method for deciding what you owe, and it is not tax advice.

Start with the form, not a spreadsheet

US self-employed people who expect to owe tax as they go generally use Form 1040-ES and Publication 505. Those documents — or a CPA, EA, or tax attorney — decide whether you must pay estimated tax and how to figure it. Contractor Ledger is an organizer: income, expenses, mileage, and a transparent running workup. It does not calculate what you owe the IRS.

Form 1040-ES (2026) states a general rule that includes an informational $1,000 estimated-tax threshold. The workbook displays that threshold. It does not apply it as a yes/no for you.

The four 2026 voucher dates

As printed: April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Q3 — September 15, 2026 — is the date that lands on summer invoices. The January 15, 2027 payment is not required if you file the 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the balance with the return (form footnote). Dates, weekdays, and how to pay the IRS.

Two paths on the form (structure only)

Form 1040-ES (2026) describes, in the General Rule, paying at least the lesser of:

  • 90% of the tax shown on the current-year return, or
  • 100% of the tax shown on the prior-year return (110% if 2025 AGI was more than $150,000, or $75,000 if your 2026 status is married filing separately).

People call the second path a “safe harbor.” The names are informal. The form is the source. Contractor Ledger uses that structure as a convenience: required annual = the lesser of 90% of this organizer’s federal estimate and 100% or 110% of the 2025 tax you typed. If prior-year tax is blank or 0, only the 90% path is used. That is not a finding that you met a safe harbor.

A sketch of self-employment tax (as the form lays it out)

On Form 1040-ES (2026), net earnings from self-employment start at 92.35% of net profit. Medicare (HI) is 2.9% of that base. Social Security (OASDI) is 12.4% of the lesser of that base and the remaining wage base. Combined, those two rates are 15.3%. Half of SE tax is deductible on the SE worksheet. The 2026 Social Security maximum income printed on the form is $184,500.

The organizer follows those percents in visible Setup cells. It does not model Additional Medicare Tax (0.9%), two-spouse SE figured separately, or farming/fishing special rules.

Then a simplified income-tax workup

After half of SE tax, optional “other deductions” you type, and the 2026 standard deduction for the filing status you picked, the file reads federal income tax from a visible 2026 rate schedule copied from Form 1040-ES page 10 / Rev. Proc. 2025-32. Organizer federal = that income tax + SE tax. Credits, QBID, AMT, NIIT, itemized-deduction limitation, OBBBA-era extras, and most adjustments are omitted — not estimated at zero. They are simply not there.

2026 standard deduction amounts shipped on Setup (Form 1040-ES chart): Single $16,100; married filing separately $16,100; head of household $24,150; married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse $32,200. They are yellow. Edit them if the IRS publishes a correction.

What Contractor Ledger actually computes

  1. YTD net = logged income − deductible expenses − mileage amount.
  2. Net used = Setup expected annual net if you filled it in; otherwise YTD net. Early in the year, YTD understates a full year — type an expected net if you are planning ahead.
  3. SE base = max(0, net used) × 92.35%.
  4. Medicare = SE base × 2.9%. Social Security = min(SE base, $184,500 − optional W-2 wages) × 12.4%.
  5. Half SE deduction, standard deduction, tax from the visible bracket table.
  6. Required annual = lesser of the 90% path and the prior-year path, with the blank/zero fallback above.
  7. SEND THIS QUARTER = round(required × N/4 − payments you logged), where N is 1–4 through the next printed due date. Catch-up is built in. ROUND to whole dollars for the voucher. Not your IRS bill.

The Sample tab uses demo numbers (including a $6,400 prior-year tax and a $60,000 expected net) so you can see the formulas move. Sample does not feed Income, Expenses, Mileage, or Quarterly.

Mileage and meals change the net before any of that

2026 business standard mileage: 72.5¢ per mile January 1–June 30 (IRS IR-2025-128) and 76¢ per mile July 1–December 31 (IRS IR-2026-29). Both rates are user-editable on Setup. Do not also log actual car and truck expenses for the same vehicle and period. Meals use a Setup percent pre-filled at 50% and labeled as a user assumption — nothing is auto-halved without that cell.
