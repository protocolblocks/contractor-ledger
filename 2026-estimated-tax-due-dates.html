Where these dates come from

IRS Form 1040-ES (2026) prints the payment due dates as April 15, 2026; June 15, 2026; September 15, 2026; and January 15, 2027. Contractor Ledger copies those four dates onto Setup and Quarterly. It does not invent a fifth date, and it does not move a date because a weekend or holiday might apply.

For 2026 the four printed dates fall on weekdays (Wednesday, Monday, Tuesday, Friday). The weekend / legal-holiday next-business-day rule is described on the form and in Publication 505. This organizer does not compute that shift.

September 15, 2026

Payment 3 covers income in the period the form associates with that voucher — generally June, July, and August. If you are a 1099 contractor who invoices late in the summer, this is the date that sneaks up while people are still finding receipts. Contractor Ledger’s Quarterly tab is built so you can open the file before that date, read SEND THIS QUARTER, and then pay the IRS yourself if you decide a payment is due.

Whether you must pay, and how much, is a Form 1040-ES / Pub. 505 / tax-pro question. The $1,000 estimated-tax threshold on the form is displayed in the workbook as information only. The file does not decide that you must pay.

January 15, 2027 footnote. Form 1040-ES (2026) notes that you do not have to make the January 15, 2027 payment if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the balance with the return. Read the form. This site does not apply that footnote for you.

How to pay the IRS

Contractor Ledger cannot send money, file a voucher, or tell you that a number is correct. When you decide to pay estimated tax, the IRS describes electronic options including:

  • IRS Direct Pay — pay from a checking or savings account for the tax year and payment type listed on that page. This is how to pay the IRS, not a service we run.
  • EFTPS (Electronic Federal Tax Payment System) — a Treasury system many self-employed people enroll in so they can schedule federal payments. Enrollment is on that site, not in the workbook.

Other methods (card processors, mail with a 1040-ES voucher, and so on) are listed at IRS.gov/payments. We do not rank them.

What the workbook does with the dates

On Quarterly, SEND THIS QUARTER is the remaining required annual amount through the next (or current) printed due date, minus amounts you typed as already paid. Required annual is the lesser of 90% of this organizer’s federal estimate and 100% or 110% of the 2025 tax you typed — matching the Form 1040-ES General Rule structure, not producing your IRS bill. If prior-year tax is blank or 0, only the 90% current-year path is used.

Record what you actually sent in the yellow payment cells. The file does not know what left your bank.
